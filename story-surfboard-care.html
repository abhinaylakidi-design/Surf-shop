Look After Your Board, Session After Session

Good board care is usually quiet and repetitive. A few deliberate minutes between sessions can help you notice problems early and keep your equipment ready for the next outing.

01 /

Learn what your board is made from

Start with the manufacturer's care instructions rather than a universal routine borrowed from a different board. Soft tops, epoxy constructions, and traditional fiberglass boards can have different details, fittings, and repair requirements. Save the model information and any guidance supplied at purchase so you can find it later without guessing.

Identify the removable parts and learn which tools, if any, are appropriate. Some boards have construction-specific features that should only be handled according to their instructions. Do not tighten, loosen, seal, or modify something simply because another surfer's board has a similar-looking part. If you bought the board used and lack documentation, ask a knowledgeable shop or the manufacturer to help identify it. Knowing the construction makes every later conversation about care and repairs more useful, especially when a problem is difficult to describe.

02 /

Rinse thoughtfully and let equipment dry

After a session, remove loose sand gently and rinse as recommended for your equipment. Pay attention to areas where grit gathers around fittings and accessories. Avoid aggressive cleaning products or improvised solvents; if ordinary cleaning does not solve a problem, check the manufacturer's advice before experimenting. What works on one surface may be unsuitable for another.

Give the board and accessories an opportunity to dry in a suitable shaded, ventilated place before longer storage. A wet bag filled with sand is an unhelpful home for equipment. Empty it, shake out grit away from delicate surfaces, and allow it to dry as well. Treat the routine as a chance to slow down after the beach. You are not trying to make a working surfboard look unused; you are removing what should not remain trapped against it between outings.

03 /

Protect it from unnecessary heat and knocks

Think about where your board waits while you do something else. A parked vehicle can become very hot, and a protective bag is not a guarantee against excessive heat. Follow the manufacturer's temperature and storage guidance, and avoid leaving the board in prolonged direct sun when it is not being used. Planning where it will go during lunch is easier than solving a problem afterward.

On land, choose a stable place where the board will not fall, obstruct a walkway, or be struck by a door. Leaning a board casually against a vehicle can become expensive when someone opens it or a gust arrives. Keep pressure off vulnerable areas such as fins and rails. These habits are ordinary rather than technical, which makes them easy to overlook. Many avoidable equipment problems begin during the parts of a surf day when nobody is actually surfing.

04 /

Transport the board as carefully as you ride it

A suitable bag or cover helps protect a board during handling, but it does not replace thoughtful packing. Before loading, check that the board will not rub against sharp objects or be squeezed by luggage. Consider the path from storage to vehicle as well: narrow doors, low ceilings, and crowded parking areas all reward a little patience.

If using a roof system, follow the rack and strap manufacturer's instructions and check compatibility with your vehicle and board. Improvised tie-down arrangements are not a place to rely on guesswork. Ask someone experienced to demonstrate the correct setup if you are uncertain. Recheck the load as directed for the system you use. For travel, find out the carrier's requirements in advance and allow time for packing. A rushed departure often turns careful equipment into an afterthought.

05 /

Make inspection part of putting it away

Look over the board in good light after cleaning. Notice fresh cracks, damaged edges, loose fittings, or changes around a previous repair. Compare anything suspicious with what you remember from the last session. A quick photograph can help you explain a concern to a repair professional and track whether an existing mark has changed.

If you suspect a breach that could admit water, stop using the board and ask for construction-appropriate advice. Do not assume a sticker or an arbitrary glue makes a reliable repair. Similarly, avoid probing or cutting into a damaged area simply to find out more. Describe how the damage happened, whether the board was used afterward, and what construction you have. Clear information helps a professional decide what assessment is needed. Early questions are often easier to handle than a larger problem hidden beneath a temporary patch.

06 /

Build a routine you will actually repeat

An elaborate maintenance checklist is not helpful if it feels too demanding after every outing. Create a small sequence that fits your space: unload carefully, clean as instructed, inspect, dry, and store securely. Keep accessories together so a missing fin key or damaged leash does not become a surprise at the next beach arrival.

For a rental board, follow the provider's return instructions and report any damage honestly, even when you are not sure when it happened. For your own equipment, schedule repairs before the day you intend to use it again. A cared-for board will still collect evidence of use; the goal is attention, not perfection. The best routine makes you familiar enough with your equipment to notice when something deserves a closer look, and simple enough that you continue doing it throughout the season.

TAKE IT TO THE WATER

A few things to remember.

  • Use care instructions for your specific construction.
  • Clean, inspect, and dry before longer storage.
  • Avoid unnecessary heat, unstable resting places, and rushed transport.
  • Ask a repair professional about suspected structural damage.
